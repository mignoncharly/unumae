# Community rules

**Approved 2026-08-22, as written.** This is the text users accept before they
can enter the draw. Written to be read once, by a real person, in about ninety
seconds.

The appeals commitment in rule 8 was reviewed and kept, so it is now an
operational promise: somebody has to be available to re-review a decision.

Accepting it sets `accepted_rules_at`, which is a hard requirement for
eligibility (Article 5.1).

## Unumae — the rules

This is a small place with a simple promise: every day, one ordinary person is
introduced to the world. These rules exist to keep that possible.

### 1. There is one person here at a time. Treat them that way.

Today's Human is not content. They are somebody who agreed to be seen, once,
by strangers. They chose to do something most people would find frightening.

Ask them what you would ask a stranger you respected. If you would not say it
to them in a room, do not send it here.

### 2. Ask questions. Do not deliver verdicts.

You can ask anything. You cannot use a question to make a statement about
someone — about their country, their religion, their body, their politics, or
their life.

The difference is usually obvious:

> "What do people misunderstand about where you live?" — a question.
> "Why is your country like that?" — a verdict wearing a question mark.

We remove the second kind. Not because the topic is forbidden, but because it
was not really a question.

### 3. Nobody here is competing.

There are no followers, no scores, no rankings, and no way to pay for
attention. If you are here to build an audience, this is the wrong place, and
nothing we add later will change that.

Do not use questions, portraits or reports to promote anything — yourself, a
business, a cause, or another platform.

### 4. Tell the truth about who you are.

Do not create or operate duplicate accounts, present yourself as somebody else,
or enter the draw on behalf of anyone but yourself. Account, provider, and
device checks raise the cost of duplicates; they do not prove global human
uniqueness.

If you are selected, the story you tell must be your own. You may leave out
anything you like — that is your right — but what you do say has to be true.

### 5. Some things are never allowed.

- Harassment, threats, or targeting a person or group
- Hate directed at people for who they are
- Sexual content, and any sexual content involving minors, which we report to
  the authorities
- Encouraging self-harm or suicide
- Sharing anyone's private information, including Today's Human's — where they
  live, work, or can be found
- Trying to contact Today's Human outside this app after seeing them here

### 6. Protect the person, not the platform.

If something here worries you, report it. Reports are read by a person.

Reporting is free, always available, and never held against you. If you are
unsure whether something crosses a line, report it and let us decide — that is
our job, not yours.

### 7. If you are selected

You will be asked, and you can say no. Declining costs you nothing and does not
affect any future draw.

If you accept:

- You choose what to share. Every part of the portrait beyond the minimum is
  optional, and no question has to be answered.
- Your country is shown. Your city and your exact age are yours to withhold.
- A person reviews your portrait before it is published.
- You can ask to be removed from the Archive afterwards, and we will do it.

### 8. What happens when rules are broken

In order: content is removed, then the account is suspended, then banned.
Serious harm — threats, sexual content involving minors, coordinated
harassment — skips straight to the end and, where required, to the authorities.

If you are banned you are out of every future draw. If you were previously
published, your Archive entry is removed.

You can dispute a decision. Say so and a different person will look at it.

### 9. These rules can change. The promise cannot.

We will update this page as we learn. What cannot change is underneath it: no
followers, no rankings, no way to buy a better chance, and one ordinary person
a day.

## Notes on the draft — not part of the user-facing text

Decisions taken while drafting. All five were reviewed and kept:

1. **Rule 2 is unusual and deliberate.** Most platforms ban topics. This bans a
   *form* — the rhetorical question used as an accusation — which is the actual
   mechanism by which "just asking questions" becomes harassment. It is harder
   to moderate consistently than a topic ban, and worth the difficulty.
2. **No prohibition on discussing politics, religion or nationality.** A
   product introducing strangers across the world cannot forbid the subjects
   those strangers differ on. Rule 2 constrains how, not what.
3. **Rule 5 forbids contacting Today's Human off-platform.** Unenforceable
   directly, but it makes the norm explicit, which matters when the person is
   findable elsewhere.
4. **Appeals are promised in rule 8.** That is a real operational commitment —
   somebody has to actually be available to re-review. If you would rather not
   promise it before launch, that sentence should come out now rather than be
   quietly broken later.
5. **Length.** Around 700 words. Longer than most people read, shorter than
   anything legally exhaustive. This is the norms document; the Terms of
   Service is a separate legal one, still to be written.
